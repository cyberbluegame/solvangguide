Velkommen til Danish Days!

A three-day heritage festival in the Danish Capital of America — pastries, parades, Viking living history, and Santa Ynez Valley wine.

Solvang was founded in 1911 by Danish-American educators and church leaders who wanted a college town that felt like home. They planted a Danish folk school on the Santa Ynez Valley floor, then built half-timbered shops, storks, and windmills that still define Mission Drive. What began as a farming village became California’s most recognizable Scandinavian streetscape — and Danish Days is the weekend the village tells that story out loud.

The festival has run since 1936, when locals first dressed in folk costume, rolled æbleskiver in the street, and invited the valley to celebrate Danish heritage. The 89th Annual Solvang Danish Days, September 18–20, 2026, keeps that compact, walkable format: free admission, three major parades, a Viking encampment in Solvang Park, folk music on the Midtbyen Stage, and pastry lines that start before the bakeries unlock the doors.

What makes it special is how local it still feels. You will hear Danish spoken at the æbleskiver tents, see folk dancers in embroidered bodices, and watch a torchlight Viking procession after dusk on Friday. You will also eat like a valley regular: jam-filled pancake balls, medisterpølse, kringle from the old bakeries, and Santa Ynez wines poured a few steps from Copenhagen Drive. Book lodging early — Danish Days is one of the few weekends that truly sells Solvang out.

Free admission all weekend. The village is compact: park once, walk Mission Drive and Copenhagen Drive, and plan breakfast lines on Saturday and Sunday.

Complete 3-Day Festival Schedule

The full hour-by-hour plan is open below — no clicking required. Times are local Pacific Time and may shift slightly; arrive early for parades and the æbleskiver breakfast.

🇩🇰 Opening Night

Friday, September 18, 2026

Marketplace stalls, the first concert, the 2026 Danish Days Maid, and the torchlight parade that turns Copenhagen Drive into a river of lanterns.

  • 4:00 PM

    🇩🇰 Velkommen / Danish Days Artisan Marketplace

    Woodworkers, textile artists, and Danish-import booths open on Copenhagen Drive as the street closes to through traffic. This is the quietest hour to browse before Saturday crowds.

  • 5:00 PM

    🎺 Free Concert & Opening Ceremony

    Midtbyen Stage welcome, folk-dance demonstration, and introduction of the 2026 Danish Days Maid. Bring a light layer — mid-September evenings cool quickly in the valley.

  • 7:30 PM

    🔥 Torchlight Procession

    Costumed walkers, shields, and lanterns move through midtown. Claim a curb on Copenhagen Drive or Mission Drive by 7:00 PM. This is the most photogenic night of the weekend.

🎺 Main Festival Day

Saturday, September 19, 2026

Breakfast queues, Viking living history, eating contests, the grand parade, then a beer-and-wine garden under the oaks.

  • 8:30 AM – 12:30 PM

    🥞 Æbleskiver Breakfast

    World-famous breakfast at Copenhagen Drive & Alisal Road. Cast-iron spheres, raspberry jam, and powdered sugar. Lines move, but the first sitting is the shortest wait.

  • 10:00 AM – 5:00 PM

    ⚔️ Viking Encampment & Forge Demos

    Living-history exhibit in Solvang Park — forge demos, period tents, and hands-on history for kids who have already eaten three æbleskiver.

  • 11:30 AM & 2:30 PM

    🥞 Æbleskiver Breakfast & Eating Contests

    Five rounds of fierce, powdered-sugar competition. Spectators pack the rails — stand on the Alisal side for a clear view.

  • 2:30 PM

    🎺 Danish Days Grand Parade (Floats & Folk Dancers)

    Floats, marching bands, Danish folk dancers, and equestrian units along the village core. Arrive 30–45 minutes early for a shaded curb.

  • 5:00 PM – 9:00 PM

    🍷 Beer & Wine Garden & Live Concerts

    Local Santa Ynez wines poured beside Carlsberg and festival food. Adult evening energy after the family parade hour.

🚲 Grand Finale

Sunday, September 20, 2026

Another æbleskiver breakfast, a kids’ zone, the children’s parade, and closing raffles before the village exhales.

  • 8:30 AM – 12:30 PM

    🥞 Æbleskiver Breakfast

    Same Copenhagen & Alisal corner, slightly shorter lines than Saturday if you arrive at open.

  • 10:00 AM – 4:00 PM

    🚲 Children’s Parade & Family Activities

    Kid’s Korner & Lego Activity Area — free crafts, storytelling, and a place for small legs to rest between parades.

  • 2:00 PM

    🚲 Children’s Parade

    Costumes, decorated bikes, and pets. The sweetest, slowest parade of the weekend — stand near Solvang Park.

  • 3:30 PM

    🇩🇰 Closing Ceremonies & Raffle Winners

    Midtbyen Stage wrap-up. If you are driving home to Santa Barbara or Los Angeles, give yourself extra time — Mission Drive stays busy until late afternoon.

Insider Tips for Visitors

September in the Santa Ynez Valley is warm afternoons, cool nights, and a village that is easier on foot than in a car.

Parking & shuttles

Use free public lots on the edge of downtown (Alisal Road corridor and lots behind Mission Drive) and walk in. Do not circle Copenhagen Drive looking for a parade-day space. If a festival shuttle is posted at your hotel or the Buellton park-and-ride, take it — Saturday from 1:00–4:00 PM is the hardest hour to park.

What to wear

Mid-September days often sit in the mid-70s to low-80s °F, with evenings in the 50s. Wear walking shoes, sun protection for the parade curb, and a light jacket for the Friday torchlight procession. Folk costume is welcome; sneakers are more useful than heels on brick and cobble.

Family day vs. adult night

Mornings are for æbleskiver, the Viking encampment, Kid’s Korner, and the children’s parade. After 5:00 PM Saturday the wine-and-beer garden and concerts take over. If you have small kids, plan a hotel with a pool and an early dinner, then a quiet Sunday breakfast before the 2:00 PM children’s parade.

Pair the festival with Los Olivos tasting rooms (a 10-minute drive) or a Buellton pinot stop on the way home. For a full valley plan, use our Wine & Experiences page — then come back to downtown when the windmill lights come on.
